# CEO Round 1 — challenges, tension adjudication, blind spots

## TL;DR (5 sentences max)

All four advisors are right about something, none of them have run a team shaped like ours, and three of them are quietly arguing for their own franchise rather than our north-star goals. The dominant risk for specflow is not under-tooling — it's shipping a sophisticated harness that a solo docs creator can't operate alone and a brand-new AI dev can't trust on day one. Pocock's grilling and feedback-loop discipline is the cheapest, surest win for "fewer errors"; Medin's PIV phase boundaries are the cheapest win for "shorter time"; everything else is downstream and most of it is premature. Saraev and Karpathy's autoresearch worldview only applies to a small fraction of our skill catalog and selling it as the flagship would be a category error for a docs-creator-led team building unverifiable product. The roadmap leans Pocock-substrate + Medin-spine in Phase 1–2, autoresearch and parallelism only after the substrate works.

## Pocock — what doesn't survive scrutiny

- **Weakest claim**: *"Skip reviewing the auto-written PRD — LLM summarization is good enough, the review work is done in grilling"* (KP-16, KP-20). This generalises from his own seniority. He can skip PRD review safely because he knows what to grill for and would catch a misalignment in conversation. A docs creator working alone with no senior-engineer pattern-matching, and AI-novice devs who haven't seen 50 misaligned PRDs, do not have that radar. Skipping the artifact review removes the safety net at exactly the moment our team needs one most.
- **Sharp follow-ups**:
  1. If grilling fully substitutes for PRD review, why does your `to-prd` skill produce a structured PRD at all? What is it for, if not human inspection?
  2. How does a junior dev — who didn't attend the grilling session — get aligned before implementing? The grilling Q&A is the docs creator's artifact; the implementer needs something they can trust without re-running it.
  3. You say "the work is in QA" — but QA happens after implementation cost is sunk. If the brief is wrong and grilling didn't catch it, the PRD review is the second-cheapest catch point. Why throw it away?
- **Glossing over**: Pocock's whole stack assumes a senior engineer is in the chair. Our docs creator is not an engineer; our devs are not senior. "Trust LLM summarization" is a senior's confidence move. He also under-weights handoffs *between people*, because he runs solo. PRDs that look like personal notes from a smart engineer to themselves do not survive a PM → docs creator → dev handoff.

## Medin — what doesn't survive scrutiny

- **Weakest claim**: *"The harness IS the product — ship `depends_on:` / `trigger_rule:` / `loop … until: APPROVED` YAML primitives, encode the team's SDLC as a DAG"* (KP-29, KP-30). This is exactly the failure mode Medin himself calls out in others (KP-35: don't adopt BMAD/Spec Kit/GSD wholesale because they force teams to change how they work). A YAML DAG harness with declarative gates is BMAD-shaped. specflow can absorb the *patterns* (PIV, fresh context, path-scoped rules) without becoming an Archon competitor — and the moment we ship a workflow YAML schema, we've lost the plot Pocock correctly identifies.
- **Sharp follow-ups**:
  1. Show the exact moment the docs creator — alone, no senior present — debugs a failed `trigger_rule: one_success` in our YAML. What's the diagnostic ladder? Be specific.
  2. PIV says "fresh session reads only the plan." If the plan is wrong (a real failure mode you acknowledge in KP-32), the implementer cannot see it because they have no chat history to push back from. Doesn't this *entrench* misalignment rather than catch it?
  3. You explicitly recommend `ask_user_question` for multi-choice clarity (KP-11). On a Max subscription that hits weekly limits Tuesday (KP-95), what's the token cost of structured clarifying tools across a quarter, and have you measured it?
- **Glossing over**: Medin runs a content business with parallel agents as content. His "37% of 5-hour limit" demo is a one-shot showpiece. He waves at AI-novice teams but every primitive he ships assumes a YAML-fluent operator. He also underweights *deletion* — every artifact is "durable memory" in his frame, which means specflow under his guidance accumulates plans, PRDs, postmortems forever. Pocock's doc-rot warning (KP-69) is real and Medin's response ("enriched commits and dated archive folders") is a deferral, not an answer.

## Saraev — what doesn't survive scrutiny

- **Weakest claim**: *"Ship `/optimize` as the flagship — autoresearch every skill in the catalog for ~$200, every dollar compounds"* (KP-21, KP-23, KP-28). This applies only where binary verifiable evals exist (KP-27 — his own caveat). The skills that actually drive specflow's value — `/grill`, plan-feature, design tradeoffs, QA judgment, simplify-with-taste — are precisely the ones autoresearch can't legitimately optimize without an LLM-judge that will reward-hack (KP-22). Saraev's diagram-skill demo (32/40 → 39/40, $10) works because diagrams have legible-text/palette/flow as binary axes. Try optimizing a grill skill against an LLM judge and you get a skill that produces conversations the judge likes, not conversations that catch real misalignments.
- **Sharp follow-ups**:
  1. Name three specflow skills today where you can write a binary, non-gameable eval *that doesn't reduce to "an LLM thinks it's good"*. If the answer is fewer than three, what does `/optimize` actually optimize?
  2. Your "decreasing human involvement is monotonic" claim (KP-111) is in direct conflict with Karpathy's own jagged-intelligence claim (KP-63), which you cite approvingly elsewhere. Which is it?
  3. Multi-provider mirror (CLAUDE.md → AGENTS.md → GEMINI.md) is cheap insurance. But cross-provider review (Codex reviews Claude) requires the team to maintain skills across two harnesses. For a solo docs creator, that's twice the upkeep. What's the actual ROI for a 1+small-team config, not a content-creator demo?
- **Glossing over**: Saraev sells autoresearch because autoresearch sells YouTube views. Strip the metrics theatre and the actually-deliverable advice for our team is: write a CLAUDE.md with a Lab Notes line; pin Haiku for triage; don't build agent org charts. That's three lines of advice, not a flagship feature. He also waves at security (Mol Bot, axolotl-password leak) but doesn't acknowledge that for a 1-person ops team, the operational tax of multi-provider posture *is the security risk* — more surface area, more keys, more JSONL files leaking.

## Karpathy — what doesn't survive scrutiny

- **Weakest claim**: *"Treat the spec as primary, the codebase as downstream rendering — invest the docs creator's hours in spec quality and accept that code under those specs is partially disposable"*. This is Karpathy reasoning from the autoresearch repo (a verifiable domain) to a general SDLC. Pocock's empirical pushback (KP-101: "I tried this. I really tried it. It sucks.") is data. Karpathy's response is to invoke "deep modules" — but deep modules don't make spec-driven *regeneration* safe; they just make spec-driven *intent* legible. The two are different claims and Karpathy elides them.
- **Sharp follow-ups**:
  1. Name a real product (not a research repo, not a kernel benchmark) where spec-as-primary and code-as-disposable held up over 12 months and three feature waves. If you can't, why are you prescribing it for our docs creator?
  2. "Verifiability decides automation speed" is empirically true. Now: estimate the percentage of specflow's surface today that is verifiable vs unverifiable. Be specific. If it's <30%, what is the autoresearch worldview actually doing for us this quarter?
  3. You cite jagged intelligence (KP-63) and Stripe-by-email as a design-level error a human wouldn't make. That same jagged model is the one you'd let "NEVER STOP" in autoresearch. How do you square those for a small team that can't watch the loop?
- **Glossing over**: Karpathy reasons from a researcher's pulpit. He has never been the only person who can answer a domain question on a Tuesday morning. Spec-as-leverage is exactly the framing that breaks when the spec author is the docs creator and they're on a flight. His autoresearch case study is one researcher with locked eval files, not a team where the eval surface IS the codebase being mutated for clients with deadlines. He also under-weights persona/teammate dynamics (which he labels rounding error in KP-89) because he doesn't manage humans.

## Adjudication of the 10 headline tensions

### Tension 1 — Should the docs creator review PRDs/issues, or skip and trust LLM summary?
- **Position**: Depends. Skip *issue-level* review, keep *PRD-level* review (lightweight: skim + verifiable AC + unknowns).
- **Why**: Our docs creator does not have Pocock's grilling-radar internalised. PRD review is a 5-minute insurance against a 5-hour wrong implementation. But Pocock is right that issues should not be polished — they're a byproduct. Compromise hits all three goals: fewer errors (PRD scan catches misalignment), shorter time (no issue-polishing ritual), more productive (review-once at the right altitude). Bake "verifiable acceptance criteria" into PRD review per Karpathy — the review *is* writing the AC, not editing prose.

### Tension 2 — Keep PRDs in repo, or delete after merge?
- **Position**: Archive, don't delete; do not auto-load into context.
- **Why**: Pocock's doc-rot worry is real (old PRDs misalign future agents). Medin's audit-trail worry is also real (a junior dev needs to see "why did we build it this way"). Resolution: dated `active/specs/archive/` folder, never path-scoped-loaded by default, scout sub-agent decides if relevant. The docs creator's holiday backup needs the archive; the running session shouldn't pay context tax for it. Goal alignment: archive supports knowledge transfer (blind spot below), exclusion supports fewer errors.

### Tension 3 — Specs as source of truth, or code as ground truth?
- **Position**: Code is ground truth; spec is intent. Pocock wins this one.
- **Why**: Our team is building product, not a research artifact. Karpathy's frame works in verifiable domains we mostly don't operate in. Treating the running code + manual QA as ground truth means the docs creator's job is "shape intent and verify the rendering," not "maintain a parallel canonical reality." Direct hit on goal 2 (shorter time — no spec-bookkeeping tax) and goal 3 (fewer errors — single source of truth).

### Tension 4 — Autonomous loops: NEVER-STOP or human gates?
- **Position**: Depends, but default to gates. Karpathy's NEVER-STOP is safe iff (a) fixed scalar metric, (b) read-only eval surface, (c) git-as-checkpoint. None of our current pipeline meets all three.
- **Why**: For an AI-novice team, the failure cost of a runaway loop (Saraev's "rm -rf from a Twitter thread" surface, KP-109) dwarfs the productivity benefit of removing gates. specflow should ship gated-by-default and *earn* the right to remove a gate per skill by demonstrating a verifiable judge. This is Pocock's HITL/AFK frontmatter idea in operational form.

### Tension 5 — QA fully automated or human re-entry point?
- **Position**: Human. Pocock right, Medin's Dark Factory wrong for our team.
- **Why**: Our team is small, our product is unverifiable in the dimensions that matter (taste, UX, brand fit). Saraev's "taste is a metric you haven't bothered to define" is academically defensible and operationally bankrupt for a docs-creator-led product. Manual QA is also where the docs creator re-enters the loop after a fan-out — protecting goal 3 (fewer errors) and serving as a learning surface for AI-novice devs (they watch QA findings become rules). QA findings feed new issues; they are not the end.

### Tension 6 — `ask_user_question` tool or plain conversation?
- **Position**: Plain conversation for grilling; structured tool for short clarifications mid-execution.
- **Why**: Grilling is a 22–80 question conversation — JSON wrapping every turn is real cost on a Max subscription that hits weekly limits (KP-95). But a single mid-implementation "did you want sorted ascending or descending?" deserves the structured choice. The split costs nothing and dodges both camps' worst case.

### Tension 7 — Agent org charts or two simple patterns?
- **Position**: Two patterns only. Saraev right.
- **Why**: Multiplicative reliability (0.9⁵ = 0.59) is real. Anthropomorphic CEO/CMO chart is theatre. Function-based fan-outs (multi-reviewer-by-dimension) are fine — they aren't org charts, they're specialised judges. The line: by-function good, by-role bad.

### Tension 8 — Heavyweight framework, own thin harness, or extend Archon?
- **Position**: Own thin harness. specflow IS that harness. Don't import Archon's YAML.
- **Why**: We are already building specflow. Adopting Archon would be a re-platform, and shipping our own DAG schema turns us into mini-Archon. Steal patterns (PIV phase boundaries, path-scoped rules, postmortem skill) without the YAML DSL. Inspect-end-to-end is the non-negotiable for an AI-novice team.

### Tension 9 — Replace Claude Code's plan mode or coexist?
- **Position**: Wrap, don't replace. Grill *precedes* plan mode and refuses to let plan mode produce an asset until grilling Q&A exists.
- **Why**: Replacing fights the host harness; wrapping uses it. The discriminator is the gate: no `.specflow/discoveries/<slug>.md` → plan mode is blocked. This is a 30-line skill that ships as a plugin entry point and gives the docs creator a single command (`/grill`) as their day-1 mental model.

### Tension 10 — Ship full parallel infra (worktrees + DB branches + ports) or stay lean?
- **Position**: Stay lean in Phase 1–2; ship worktree script in Phase 3 only when the team has a backlog wider than one human reviewer can serve.
- **Why**: KP-80 — invest in parallel infra when *you* are the bottleneck, not before. For a docs creator + small dev team starting out, parallel agents are a tax, not a multiplier — DB branching is moot (we have no DB yet) and four worktrees mean four QA contexts the docs creator can't keep in their head. Build the thin `w` script when the dev team complains about reviewer pile-up, not before.

## What none of them addressed

- **The docs creator is a single point of failure with no second.** None of the advisors run a team where one person owns specs, glossary, grilling, archive, and QA. When they go on holiday, the system stops. specflow needs (a) a `team-handoff` skill that produces a "what's in flight, what's blocked, where artifacts live" snapshot, and (b) a CLAUDE.md section labelled "what to do if the docs creator is unreachable" — concrete escalation path, not "ask Claude harder."
- **Trust calibration is not a slogan, it's a curve over weeks.** AI-novice devs need a progressive ladder: week 1 Claude only suggests, week 2 Claude implements with reviewer, week 3 Claude implements + self-reviews, week 4 fan-out. None of the advisors describe this curve because they are all already at the end of it. specflow should ship a `getting-started` profile that is *intentionally restrictive* and unlocks via an explicit upgrade ritual. Saraev's "agents have no agency by default" (KP-108) is the inverse problem; ours is "novice has no calibration by default."
- **People-to-people handoffs, not just session-to-session.** PIV is "session A writes plan.md, session B reads it." Our reality is "PM grilled docs creator on Tuesday, docs creator wrote PRD on Wednesday, dev picks it up on Thursday, reviewer is unavailable until Monday." Each of those is a *human* handoff with a context decay. None of the advisors built primitives for this — Medin's enriched commits come closest but don't cover pre-implementation. specflow needs a `handoff-note` skill that runs at the *human* boundary, not the session boundary, and a "stale" tag on artifacts older than N days.
- **Cost reality on a Max subscription, not unlimited compute.** Three of four advisors run paid-by-token-or-content. Cole's "37% of limit running 4 parallel" is a flex, not a baseline. We need a hard rule that pins Haiku for triage, Sonnet default, Opus only when explicitly requested, and a `/budget` skill that warns at 60% weekly. Multi-provider mirror is interesting but the operational tax is real for a 1-person ops team — don't ship it Phase 1.
- **Failure recovery at 2am.** "Agent runs amok" is glossed past. None of the advisors describe a `panic` skill: kill all background agents, rewind worktrees to last green, snapshot what each agent did, surface in a one-page incident note. This belongs *before* parallel infra, not after.
- **Onboarding day-1 with no senior present.** A new dev opens the repo, runs `/init`, and hits — what? The advisors assume a senior can mentor. specflow should ship a `tour` skill that produces a 10-minute walkthrough specific to *this* repo's CLAUDE.md, glossary, active skills, and "things you cannot touch yet."
- **When to override the AI.** AI-novice devs default to either rubber-stamping or distrusting. specflow needs a small `confidence-check` ritual: "before merging, list one thing the AI did that you don't fully understand. If you can't name one, you didn't read the diff." Karpathy's jagged-intelligence point becomes an operational habit, not a slogan.
- **Non-engineers in the loop (designers, PMs).** None of the advisors describe a designer interacting with specflow. Our team likely has them. Skills should be invocable from *prose intent* without learning slash command syntax — a `/idea` entry point that routes to the right skill. (This is also Karpathy's voice/dictation point, KP-19, made operational.)
- **The plugin owner is also the dogfood team.** specflow's docs creator is also the only person maintaining specflow. Every advisor's "the plugin gets better with use" assumes someone is curating it. We need a quarterly `prune` ritual or specflow accumulates skills nobody uses and rules nobody enforces.
- **Phase fit with our PRD (Phase 1 light foundation, Phase 2 develop skill, Phase 3 memory loop)**: not a single advisor mapped their advice to our phasing. Karpathy and Saraev's autoresearch is Phase 3 at earliest. Medin's PIV is Phase 1–2. Pocock's grill + feedback-loop audit is Phase 1. The advisors all want their bet to be Phase 1; only Pocock's actually fits.

## Where I'm leaning for specflow's roadmap

**Phase 1 (light foundation) is Pocock-substrate.** Ship `/grill` as the canonical discovery phase, wrapping (not replacing) Claude Code's plan mode and refusing to let it produce an asset until Q&A exists. Ship a `feedback-loop-audit` skill that runs once per repo to score test/typecheck/e2e fitness for AI use. Ship a minimal `CONTEXT.md` glossary skill. Ship a `team-handoff` skill that produces a "what's in flight" snapshot — the single primitive that protects against the docs-creator-as-single-point-of-failure blind spot. HITL/AFK frontmatter on every skill from day one. No YAML DAG, no parallel infra, no autoresearch. Pocock's bet is the only one that matches Phase 1's "light foundation" framing, and the only one that doesn't require the team to learn a new mental model first.

**Phase 2 (develop skill) is Medin-spine without the YAML.** PIV phase boundaries enforced as fresh-session conventions (not declarative DAG): plan-feature writes `.specflow/plans/<slug>.md`, execute reads only that file, review runs in a third fresh session reading only the diff. Path-scoped rules (`.claude/rules/*.md` with `paths:` frontmatter) for tier-2 context — explicitly leveraging an existing Claude Code primitive rather than inventing one. Per-node model selection wired into skill frontmatter (Haiku/Sonnet/Opus declared per skill). Postmortem skill that turns escaped bugs into rule edits. This phase teaches the team the discipline; it does not require them to operate a YAML harness.

**Phase 3 (memory loop) is selective Karpathy + Saraev, not flagship autoresearch.** Lab Notes section in CLAUDE.md from the start (cheap, compounding), then in Phase 3 a thin `/optimize` skill scoped *only* to skills with binary verifiable acceptance — `simplify`, `format`, `release-version-check`, possibly `tdd`. Cross-provider Codex review for high-stakes PRs as a Phase 3 opt-in (Codex plugin already exists, low marginal cost). Worktree-per-task script gated on the "humans bottleneck on review" trigger (KP-80). Confidence-check, panic, and tour skills introduced here as the trust ladder matures. **Update conditions**: this leans changes if (a) we measure that >50% of specflow skills are in fact verifiable (then Saraev moves up), (b) the team grows past 3 devs (then parallel infra moves up), or (c) a real outage proves multi-provider posture is worth the operational tax.
